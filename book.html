Overview

The Spectral Theorem: Foundations, Applications, Modern Perspective is an 11-page mathematical monograph by Benas Petronis. It builds the language needed for spectral theory from the ground up: inner-product spaces and induced norms, orthogonality and projections, Cauchy sequences, completeness, and Hilbert spaces.

The manuscript then develops orthonormal sets, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, Fourier series, and the projection theorem before turning to linear operators. It introduces adjoints and self-adjoint operators, explains finite-dimensional diagonalization through eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and contrasts that with the continuous and discrete spectrum in infinite dimensions. The closing perspective presents spectral decomposition using projection-valued measures, including the operator integral that represents an operator as a weighted accumulation of projections across its spectrum.

Central idea: The spectral theorem connects the geometry of Hilbert spaces with the structure of self-adjoint operators, moving from familiar matrix diagonalization to the measure-theoretic language required in infinite-dimensional analysis.
